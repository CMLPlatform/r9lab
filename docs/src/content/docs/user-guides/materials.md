title: Record materials
description: Note what a product or component is made of, with quantities, as a bill of materials.
---

A bill of materials lists the materials in a product or component, each with a quantity. It is
what makes records comparable across products.

## What you need

- A product or component record.
- A weight for each material, or a good estimate marked as one.
- A record you own.
- Evidence for the material: a label, a marking such as `>PP<`, or a test.

## Steps

1. Open the record you own and choose **Edit product** (or **Edit component**).
1. In **Properties**, find **Materials**.
1. Choose **Add material**. Search the picker and choose the material. If two entries share a
   name, the source in brackets tells them apart.
1. Enter the **Quantity**. It must be above 0.
1. Pick the unit. It starts at **g**. You can also use **kg**, **m** or **cm**.
1. Choose **Add**.

To change a row, edit its quantity or pick another unit. To remove a row, choose **Remove** and
confirm. Changes to materials save as you make them.

## Warnings

The app flags these but does not block you. A duplicate is the exception: the picker does not
offer it.

- **No materials or components.** A record needs at least one of them.
- **Duplicate material.** A material can appear once per record. A material already in the list is
  not offered in the picker. Change the quantity of the existing row instead.
- **Mass balance.** For a record without components, the materials should add up to its weight
  within 10%. A larger gap is a prompt to re-check.
- **Total mass.** The app adds up the rows only when all of them are in g or kg. If one row is in
  m or cm, it says the total is not available.

## What good data looks like

- Materials come from the shared list. If none fits, leave it out rather than pick the closest.
- Quantities use a mass unit (g or kg) where you can. Rows in m or cm are for lengths such as cable.
- Uncertain materials say so in the notes, such as "Likely polypropylene, unconfirmed".
- A measured quantity is not mixed up with an estimate. Say which one it is.
